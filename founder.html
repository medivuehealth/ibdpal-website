The Founder

Aryan Shashi Kumar

Founder, MediVue & IBDPal

Why IBDPal exists

Every lasting program begins with a simple standard: make tomorrow a little clearer than today.

Aryan Shashi Kumar founded IBDPal around a belief that people navigating Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis deserve education and tools that respect their time, their intelligence, and the complexity of life between clinic visits.

IBD information is everywhere, but useful answers are often scattered across research papers, clinical resources, organizational websites, and countless search results. Aryan saw an opportunity to bring those pieces closer together: clear education, practical tools, trusted resources, and technology designed around questions patients actually ask.

What began as an iOS app has grown into a broader nonprofit education platform through MediVue. Today, IBDPal brings together patient guides, educational articles, nutrition resources, support information, interactive tools, and a growing library of trusted external resources.

Building with a purpose

Aryan approaches IBDPal with a simple question: Will this make something more useful or understandable for the person on the other side of the screen?

That question shapes the platform.

A guide should turn a complicated topic into something someone can finish and understand. A support page should lead to real resources. A tracking tool should help organize information for a future conversation with a care team. And technology should make health information clearer, not more overwhelming.

IBDPal is therefore built around a few consistent principles: write clearly, cite credible sources, distinguish education from medical advice, seek feedback, and keep patient education freely accessible.

From an app to an educational platform

As IBDPal has grown, so has its scope.

The platform now extends beyond digital tracking to a growing educational library, patient guides, nutrition resources, state-by-state support information, research-informed content, and interactive tools. IBDPal also connects readers with education from established organizations and institutions, including licensed resources where authorization has been provided.

But growth is not measured simply by how many pages are published.

For Aryan, progress means creating something that leaves a visitor better equipped than when they arrived: with a clearer explanation, a better question for their care team, a useful resource, or a practical next step.

Building forward

IBDPal is still being built.

New questions create new resources. Reader searches reveal gaps. Feedback leads to improvements. Research changes, and educational content must continue changing with it.

Aryan’s long-term vision is to continue developing IBDPal as an accessible nonprofit resource where thoughtful technology and understandable health education can exist in the same place.

The standard remains the same one that started the project: Make the next step a little clearer.
