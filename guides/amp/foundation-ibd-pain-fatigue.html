Sources of pain in IBD

Inflammation, strictures, abscesses, arthritis, and functional pain can coexist.

Location and timing help clinicians differentiate causes. Symptom patterns tracked over several days are more useful to your clinician than a single snapshot.

Do not assume all pain equals active colitis without evaluation. Discuss how this topic applies to your current disease activity with your gastroenterologist. Logging patterns in IBDPal or a notebook helps clinicians see trends beyond a single visit. Your GI team can adjust recommendations based on labs, imaging, and symptom trends. Second opinions are reasonable when plans feel unclear or symptoms persist despite treatment. If symptoms worsen while you try these steps, contact your clinic using your flare pathway. Fatigue beyond feeling tired

Anemia, poor sleep, medications, depression, and inflammation contribute to fatigue.

Energy may lag even when stool frequency improves. Write down questions for your gastroenterologist before each visit so limited appointment time is used well.

Self-management strategies

Gentle activity, sleep hygiene, and pacing tasks help when clinicians approve.

Pain teams, physical therapy, and psychology add tools beyond drugs alone. Medication adherence and follow-up labs are as important as diet changes for many IBD patients.

Medication and procedural options

Treating inflammation remains foundational.

Analgesic choices must avoid NSAIDs that may worsen IBD unless clinicians agree. Tell your team about travel, work stress, sleep changes, and menstrual cycle timing when symptoms shift.

When to seek urgent care

Severe abdominal pain with fever, rigid abdomen, or vomiting needs emergency evaluation.

Sudden pain changes after surgery warrant immediate outreach. Bring prior colonoscopy, imaging, and pathology reports when seeing a new IBD specialist.

Practical tips

  • Log pain location and relation to meals.
  • Track sleep hours during fatigue spikes.
  • Ask about anemia labs when exhausted.
  • Avoid NSAIDs unless GI approves.
  • Use pain-fatigue entries in visit prep.

Common questions

Is fatigue normal in remission?

It can persist. Investigate treatable causes with your team.

Should I push through pain daily?

Pacing helps; severe pain needs medical assessment.

Do opioids treat IBD inflammation?

They mask pain and may worsen gut function. Use only as directed.
