Why prior authorization happens

Insurers often require paperwork before covering specialty IBD medications, infusions, or certain tests.

Step therapy rules may ask you to try another drug first. That can delay care if it does not match your clinician's plan.

Foundation advocacy materials explain common patient rights and appeal pathways in plain language.

How to use Foundation materials

Read the Foundation prior authorization guide on the Foundation website as the original source.

Share denial letters and appeal letter templates with your clinic's prior auth or infusion coordinator.

Track refill dates, infusion schedules, and portal messages so gaps are visible early.

What IBDPal can help with

IBDPal helps you log symptoms and medications so your team has clearer visit notes while insurance reviews continue.

We do not submit prior authorizations or negotiate with insurers on your behalf.

Practical tips

  • Save denial letters and case numbers
  • Ask who at the clinic owns prior auth follow-up
  • Note refill and infusion dates in a calendar or IBDPal

Common questions

Can Foundation materials guarantee approval?

No. They educate patients and caregivers. Coverage decisions belong to your insurer and your clinical documentation.

Should I stop medication while waiting?

Do not stop prescribed therapy without instructions from your IBD clinician. Ask the clinic what to do if a refill or infusion is delayed.
