Nutrition supports energy and nutrient status in many autoimmune conditions. It does not replace disease-modifying therapy. Use this as a starter map, then personalize with your clinician or dietitian.

Topics: autoimmune nutrition, autoimmune diet basics, anti-inflammatory autoimmune eating, autoimmune deficiency nutrition

Priorities that travel across diagnoses

Meet calorie and protein needs during flares or poor appetite.

Check and replete iron, B12, vitamin D, and other labs when ordered.

Prefer food patterns over detox products.

When elimination is reasonable

Time-boxed trials with a reintroduction plan are safer than forever restriction.

Celiac disease requires a strict gluten-free diet after proper testing.

If you also have IBD

Use IBD-specific flare textures and Foundation or AGA nutrition education alongside this overview.

Log meals and symptoms so GI and dietitian visits have data.

Practical tips

  • Ask before starting specialty supplements on immunosuppression
  • Reintroduce foods on purpose
  • Pair with research publications for evidence context

Common questions

Is there one autoimmune diet?

No. Needs differ by diagnosis, medications, surgeries, and tolerances. Marketing lists oversimplify.

Can diet put autoimmune disease in remission alone?

Diet can support comfort and nutrition. Disease control usually requires medical therapy guided by specialists.
