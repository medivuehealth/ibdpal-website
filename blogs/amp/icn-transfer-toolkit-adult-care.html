Young adults with IBD eventually transfer from pediatric to adult-centered care. The ImproveCareNow Transfer Toolkit is a 10-page document co-created by the Transition-Transfer Innovation Community, reviewed by the Patient Advisory Council and Clinical Practice Committee.

Why transition planning matters

Healthcare teams want young adults to navigate the system independently. Transition should feel seamless and happen when a patient is physically and emotionally stable, not only because they aged out of pediatrics.

Inside the toolkit

  • Skills adolescents need before transfer
  • Self-advocacy tips for appointments and insurance
  • Patient narratives with real-life encouragement
  • Considerations for clinicians and families at handoff

Download the Transfer Toolkit from the ICN tools page. Pair with college planning, ICN college handbook, and visit prep checklist so you practice carrying your own medical summary.

Why transition timing matters

Pediatric IBD programs excel at family-centered care, but adult clinics expect independent self-management. Gaps during transfer raise flare and mental health risks.

ImproveCareNow transfer toolkits outline readiness checklists around age sixteen to twenty-one depending on center policy.

Start early so skills like refilling biologics and calling nurses are practiced before the last pediatric visit.

Building an adult-ready medical summary

Compile diagnosis history, surgeries, biologic dates, vaccine records, and allergy lists in one PDF.

Request copies of recent colonoscopy and MRI reports before records lag between systems.

Know your pharmacy, infusion center, and after-hours numbers without relying on parents.

First adult GI visit expectations

Adult visits may feel shorter and less hand-holding. Bring written questions and expect direct discussion of risks.

Insurance authorizations may reset at transfer. Specialty pharmacy outreach prevents missed doses.

Mental health and reproductive planning belong on adult agendas from the start.

Family role shifts

Parents move from managers to supporters unless legal guardianship continues for other reasons.

Open family conversations reduce secrecy that leads to missed appointments or hidden symptoms.

Young adults can sign releases allowing parents limited portal access if desired.

Insurance literacy during transition

Learn prior authorization steps before parents lose dependent coverage at age limits.

Understand copay accumulator policies that affect manufacturer card benefits.

Save specialty pharmacy apps on your phone with login credentials secured.

Building an adult care team locally

Interview adult gastroenterologists about after-hours coverage and infusion access.

Transfer records six to eight weeks before last pediatric visit to avoid gaps.

Schedule first adult visit while still connected to pediatric team for warm handoff.

Building habits that last beyond a flare

Choose one practical step from this guide to practice this week rather than changing everything at once. Sustainable habits outperform short strict phases for most IBD patients.

Pair new habits with existing routines, such as taking evening meds when you brush teeth, so they survive busy school or work weeks.

Revisit your plan after travel, holidays, or medication changes because tolerance and priorities shift over time.

Coordinating care across your health team

Ask your gastroenterologist whether dietitian, mental health, physical therapy, or social work referrals would help the issues raised here.

Share updates from other specialists at GI visits so drug interactions and overlapping symptoms are reviewed in one place.

Use your patient portal to upload outside lab results and hospital records before appointments when possible.

Planning ahead when life gets busy

Pack medications, snacks, and a small symptom kit before exams, trips, or overtime weeks when routines slip first.

Identify backup clinicians or infusion centers near work, campus, or relatives in case flares occur away from home.

Discuss preventive plans with your clinician before predictable stress seasons such as finals, tax season, or postpartum return to work.

Common questions

What if I am not ready at eighteen?

Many programs extend pediatric care while building skills. Readiness matters more than birthday age.

Can I keep the same biologic through transfer?

Usually yes, but authorization and infusion site may change. Confirm before the last pediatric dose.

How do I find an adult IBD specialist?

Pediatric teams recommend colleagues; ICN lists adult centers active in quality improvement networks.
