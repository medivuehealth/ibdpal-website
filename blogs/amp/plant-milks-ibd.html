Nutrition snapshot: Plant milks

Plant milks vary widely. Almond milk is often lower calorie and lower protein. Oat milk is creamier with more carbohydrate and sometimes more fiber. Soy milk usually offers more protein. Fortified calcium and vitamin D matter if you avoid dairy.

Approximate macros (typical serving)

  • Almond milk (unsweetened, 1 cup): ~30 to 40 kcal, ~1 g protein
  • Oat milk (original, 1 cup): ~120 kcal, ~3 g protein, more carbs
  • Soy milk (unsweetened, 1 cup): ~80 kcal, ~7 g protein
  • Added sugars: Flavored cartons can add 10+ g sugar per cup

Amounts vary by brand, ripeness, cooking method, and portion. Use a label or USDA FoodData Central for exact numbers, and IBDPal food logs to compare your portion to how you felt afterward.

Micronutrients and extras people ask about

  • Calcium and vitamin D: Choose fortified cartons if dairy-free
  • Carrageenan / gums: Some people feel better on simpler ingredient lists
  • Oat fiber: Can be gentle or gassy depending on the person and serving

Flare versus remission: how many people approach plant milks

Lactose-free dairy and plant milks are both options when lactose bothers you. Oat milk is not automatically low FODMAP in large servings. Almond milk may be easier in small amounts but is a poor protein source alone.

During a flare (when your team wants gentler textures)

  • Choose unsweetened, simple-ingredient cartons
  • Start with 1/2 cup in tea, coffee, or smooth cereals
  • If gums bother you, trial brands without carrageenan
  • Do not rely on almond milk alone for protein during weight loss

In remission (when variety is usually easier)

  • Compare oat, soy, and lactose-free dairy for satiety and stools
  • Use fortified soy or pea milks when protein goals are high
  • Watch barista oat milks with extra oils if fatty meals trigger you

If you have strictures, recent surgery, or a short bowel history, fiber, skins, seeds, and chunky textures may need a different plan. Follow your clinician, not a generic list.

Prep ideas that often feel kinder

Shake cartons well. Warm gently rather than boiling if separation bothers you. In smoothies, pair plant milk with banana or protein powder your dietitian approves.

Common myths about plant milks and IBD

  • Plant milk is always healthier than dairy. Depends on protein, fortification, additives, and your tolerance.
  • Oat milk is low residue for everyone. Serving size and brand fiber differ.
  • Almond milk fixes inflammation. It is a beverage choice, not a biologic.

How to track plant milks with IBDPal

Log the form (fresh vs canned, cooked texture, sweetener type, brand), portion size, and symptoms for 24 to 48 hours. Patterns beat memory at clinic visits. See tracking food and symptoms.

Questions for your gastroenterologist or dietitian

  • Which fortified milk alternative fits my calcium and protein targets?
  • Should I avoid gums or carrageenan based on my symptoms?
  • How do oat milk portions fit my FODMAP plan?

Bring your food log and recent labs so advice matches disease location, medicines, and nutrition gaps.

When food questions become urgent

Contact your care team promptly for severe pain, vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, heavy bleeding, fever, or rapid weight loss. See flare help and when to go to the ER.
