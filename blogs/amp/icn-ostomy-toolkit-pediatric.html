Ostomy surgery can feel frightening, but many young people thrive with support and honest information. The ImproveCareNow Ostomy Toolkit was created by the ICN Patient Advisory Council Advocacy Taskforce with the wider ICN community.

Designed for and by pediatric patients

The toolkit is for youth who have had or may need ostomy surgery, whether temporary (resting) or permanent.

Topics covered

  • School and telling friends
  • Travel and bathroom planning
  • Supplies, appliances, and troubleshooting
  • Playing sports and staying active
  • Clothing choices and body image
  • Reducing fear and building awareness

Download the Ostomy Toolkit from ImproveCareNow. See also IBDPal's ostomy basics article and living with an ostomy guide.

Using ImproveCareNow ostomy resources with teens

The ImproveCareNow ostomy toolkit offers youth-friendly guides on pouch changes, school logistics, and peer stories. Parents and teens can review modules together or separately.

Pediatric wound ostomy nurses remain the primary teachers for medical technique. Toolkits supplement, not replace, hands-on training.

Share toolkit sections with school nurses so bathroom and supply storage plans stay consistent.

School, sports, and social life

504 plans can include ostomy supply storage, extra bathroom passes, and PE modifications during post-op recovery.

Athletic trainers benefit from one-page summaries about stoma protection and hydration needs.

Peer mentors through ICN communities reduce isolation when teens feel different from classmates.

Parent caregiver boundaries

Teens often want increasing privacy for pouch changes. Caregivers shift toward coaching rather than doing every step as maturity allows.

Watch for depression or eating avoidance after surgery. ICN mental health guides help families find counselors familiar with IBD.

Celebrate small milestones like first sleepover or trip without centering every conversation on the stoma.

Transition planning

Transfer toolkits help move ostomy care knowledge from pediatric to adult clinics before college or job changes.

Teens should practice ordering supplies and calling the ostomy nurse independently by late high school.

Document preferred appliance brands and skin products so adult centers stock compatible supplies.

Peer connection for teens with ostomies

ICN youth events and camp programs introduce friends who normalize pouch talk.

Video modules can be watched privately before group discussions if teens prefer.

Parents step back during peer sessions when teens ask for independence.

Coordinating school nurses and athletic staff

Provide one-page care plans listing supplies stored in nurse office and locker access rules.

Athletic trainers need stoma protection guidance before contact sports return.

Update plans after appliance brand changes so schools stock compatible backups.

Recording what works for your next visit

Keep a brief symptom and lifestyle log for one to two weeks before appointments. Note sleep, stress, meals, and bowel patterns so your clinician sees trends instead of a single bad day.

List medications, supplements, and missed doses honestly. Small adherence gaps help your GI team adjust plans faster than guessing.

Bring one prioritized question from this article so limited visit time addresses what matters most to you right now.

Building habits that last beyond a flare

Choose one practical step from this guide to practice this week rather than changing everything at once. Sustainable habits outperform short strict phases for most IBD patients.

Pair new habits with existing routines, such as taking evening meds when you brush teeth, so they survive busy school or work weeks.

Revisit your plan after travel, holidays, or medication changes because tolerance and priorities shift over time.

Coordinating care across your health team

Ask your gastroenterologist whether dietitian, mental health, physical therapy, or social work referrals would help the issues raised here.

Share updates from other specialists at GI visits so drug interactions and overlapping symptoms are reviewed in one place.

Use your patient portal to upload outside lab results and hospital records before appointments when possible.

Common questions

Is the toolkit enough for home teaching?

Use it alongside nurse visits. Videos help, but first changes need supervised practice.

Can teachers discuss ostomy openly?

Only with student and family permission. Privacy preferences vary.

Where do we find ICN ostomy updates?

ImproveCareNow.org publishes toolkit revisions. Your IBD center may link local versions.
